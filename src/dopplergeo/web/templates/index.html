Find where a recording was made, from the planes overhead

When a propeller plane flies past, its engine hum drops in pitch (the Doppler effect). The exact shape of that pitch curve depends on how far away you were and on the plane's path. dopplergeo compares the curves in your recording with the real flight paths of planes that were in the air at that time (ADS-B data), and finds the spot on the ground where the curves match best.

  1. ADS-B positions of the planes that were overhead when you recorded. Rehydrate a past day (e.g. all of Florida), or collect live before you record.

  2. Record outdoors while light planes pass, with your microphone, or import a phone video. A few minutes with two or more flyovers works best.

  3. The tool first listens for aircraft in the recording; if it hears some, you mark roughly where to search and it matches them against the flight data.

{# ------------------------------------------------------------------ RECORD #}

Record outside, ideally somewhere light planes fly over (near a small airport or a flight-training area), and keep recording through at least one full flyover (about a minute either side of the loudest moment). Two or more flyovers in one recording make the answer much more precise. The time is taken from this computer's clock, which is why the matching flight data can be found later.

Echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain are switched off: they would erase the low engine hum. Built-in laptop microphones cut low sounds; an external or phone microphone usually does better.

{# ------------------------------------------------------------------ DATA #}

Flight data says where every plane was, second by second. The tool needs data for the same time and area as your recording. There are three ways to get it:

  • Rehydrate a past day: download one or more days from the free adsb.lol archive for an area, up to a whole state such as Florida. Good for recordings already made.
  • Collect live: record flight positions as they happen, from your own ADS-B receiver (best) or the adsb.lol website. Start it before you go out to record.
  • Import receiver logs: files saved by your own tar1090 / readsb receiver.

Rehydrate a past day

Each day is one ~3.6 GB download covering the whole world, whatever area you choose; the area only decides how much is kept on disk. Source: the adsb.lol open archive (ODbL 1.0, all aircraft, one release per UTC day, published about a day later). The whole day (≈3.5 GB) is streamed and only aircraft inside the box are kept. Archive tracks are thinned (a point every ~20 s in straight flight).
